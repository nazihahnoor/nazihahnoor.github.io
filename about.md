I'm a doctoral researcher at the University of Fribourg in Switzerland, where my dissertation examines how socioeconomic position in childhood and adolescence shapes cardiovascular health in adulthood. I use causal inference methods, mostly target trial emulation and g-methods, with longitudinal cohort data.

Before Fribourg, I spent seven years at the Khazanah Research Institute in Kuala Lumpur, where I led policy research on health, labour, and social policy in Malaysia. I also held a visiting research position at the University of Queensland, and I continue to collaborate across institutions in Malaysia, Australia, Switzerland, and France.

My work sits at an awkward and productive intersection: between quantitative epidemiology and critical policy research, between the global north where I now study and the global south where I grew up and where much of my policy work still lives, between the kind of evidence journals ask for and the kind of evidence that moves policy.

Outside of research, I write occasionally about books, photography, and the odd methodological puzzle. You'll find most of that under [Reflections]({{ '/reflections/' | relative_url }}).

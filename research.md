My research asks how social and economic structures become embodied as disease, and how the methodological choices we make determine what kinds of inequalities we can see.

## Current themes

<ul class="theme-list">
  <li>
    <h3>Early-life exposures and adult cardiovascular health</h3>
    <p>My dissertation examines how adolescent exposures, including family dynamics and parenting styles, shape cardiovascular risk decades later. The empirical core is a target trial emulation using longitudinal cohort data.</p>
  </li>
  <li>
    <h3>Measuring socioeconomic inequalities in health</h3>
    <p>A methodological thread running through my work asks how different operationalisations of socioeconomic position (education, occupation, wealth, neighbourhood) produce different pictures of inequality, and what that means for policy.</p>
  </li>
  <li>
    <h3>Health and care policy in Malaysia</h3>
    <p>A continuing policy research strand carried over from my years at the Khazanah Research Institute, including an ongoing book chapter on the political economy of health and care in Malaysia.</p>
  </li>
  <li>
    <h3>Gender, labour, and trade</h3>
    <p>Earlier and ongoing work on how global trade and labour market structures shape gender and health inequalities, particularly in the Southeast Asian context.</p>
  </li>
</ul>
